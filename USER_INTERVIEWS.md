# USER_INTERVIEWS.md

## Interview Goals

The goal of these quick interviews was to understand whether AI Spend Auditor feels useful to people who use AI tools, how they think about AI tool spending, and whether the product is more useful for students, individual developers, or business users.

These were informal feedback conversations with friends/classmates. They were not formal customer discovery interviews.

---

## Questions Asked

1. Which AI tools do you currently use?
2. Do you pay for any AI tool personally, or do you use free/student plans?
3. Do you know approximately how much you spend on AI tools per month?
4. Have you ever avoided, downgraded, or stopped using an AI tool because of price?
5. Would an AI spend audit be useful for you?
6. Who do you think this tool would be useful for?
7. Would you enter your email after seeing the audit result?
8. What part of the idea feels useful, unnecessary, or confusing?

## Interview 1

**Name :** Ronak  
**Role:** Student / Developer learner  
**Company :** Personal learning  
**Date:** 2026-05-13  
**Context:** Uses AI tools for studying and programming learning.

### Notes

This user uses the free ChatGPT plan for studying and uses GitHub Copilot through the student plan, which is free for learning. Because they are not paying for AI tools, the audit did not feel personally useful to them.

### Paraphrased Quotes

> “I use ChatGPT free plan for studying.”

> “I use GitHub Copilot student plan, so it is free for me.”

> “This is not useful for me as a free user, but it may be useful for people who use AI from multiple providers or use AI in their business.”

### Most Surprising Thing

The most useful feedback was that the product is not very valuable for free-plan users. If someone does not pay for AI tools, there is no real spending pain to solve.

### What It Changed About the Product

This made the target user clearer. AI Spend Auditor should not be positioned mainly for students or casual free-plan users. It should focus on individuals, teams, founders, or businesses paying for multiple AI tools or API providers.

## Synthesis

### Common Patterns

All three users were students or learner-type users, and none of them had meaningful monthly AI spend. Because of that, the product did not feel personally useful to them.

The strongest pattern was:

> Free-plan users do not feel the pain this product is solving.

The product becomes more relevant when the user is paying for multiple tools, managing a team, or using AI APIs for a business/project.

### Surprising Contradictions

One user did not find the tool useful for themselves but still understood that it could be useful for people using multiple AI providers in a business context. This shows that the product idea may be valid, but only for a narrower audience.

Another user did not understand the idea at first, which means the landing page should explain the problem more clearly.

### Product Changes From Interviews

1. The landing page should target founders, developers, small teams, and businesses using paid AI tools.
2. The product should not be positioned for all AI users.
3. The copy should mention that free-plan users may not find much savings.
4. The result page should clearly explain how recommendations are calculated to build trust.
5. The lead capture form should remain after the result, not before it, so it does not feel like a survey-only product.

### What I Would Ask Next

These interviews were useful, but they were not from the ideal target users. Next, I would interview:

- a freelancer paying for AI tools
- a developer using Cursor/Claude/ChatGPT paid plans
- a founder or small business owner using OpenAI API
- someone managing software subscriptions for a small team

The next questions should focus more on real monthly spend, who approves tool costs, and whether the person would take action after seeing estimated savings.
