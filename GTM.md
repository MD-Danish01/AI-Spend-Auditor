# GTM.md

## Target User

The exact target user for AI Spend Auditor is a founder, CTO, Head of Engineering, or engineering manager at a seed to Series A startup with a small technical team of around 3–50 people. These teams are actively using AI tools such as Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT, OpenAI API, Anthropic API, Gemini, or v0, but they may not have a formal process for reviewing AI spend.

The strongest early user is not a large enterprise buyer. It is a startup operator who is close enough to the product and budget to care about both developer productivity and monthly software/API bills. They may already be seeing AI costs appear across multiple tools, credit cards, API dashboards, or cloud invoices, but they do not yet know whether the spend is efficient.

## What They Search or Think Before Needing This

The user may search or think things like:

- “OpenAI API bill too high”
- “reduce AI tool spend”
- “Cursor vs GitHub Copilot pricing”
- “ChatGPT Team worth it?”
- “AI tools budget startup”
- “why is our AI API cost increasing?”
- “Claude vs OpenAI API cost”
- “how much should a startup spend on AI tools?”

They may also want this tool after reviewing company card expenses, Stripe invoices, OpenAI billing dashboards, or monthly finance reports.

## Where They Hang Out Online

The best early channels are communities where founders and builders already discuss tools, costs, and AI workflows:

- Hacker News
- r/SaaS
- r/startups
- r/LocalLLaMA
- Indie Hackers
- X/Twitter AI builder and founder community
- LinkedIn posts from founders, CTOs, and engineering managers
- Startup-focused Discord and Slack groups
- College/startup networks and small founder circles

## First 100 Users in 30 Days With $0 Paid Budget

The first step would be to create one strong public example audit using a realistic AI stack, such as Cursor, ChatGPT Team, Claude, and OpenAI API. This example report can be shared as a post showing how small AI costs add up and where teams may be overspending.

Next, I would publish short teardown posts on LinkedIn and X, such as “I audited a sample 6-person startup AI stack and found $290/month in possible savings.” The post would include a screenshot of the result page and a link to run a free audit.

I would also cold DM 30–50 early-stage founders, indie hackers, and student startup builders with a short personalized message offering a free AI spend audit. Instead of asking them to try a random tool, I would frame it as: “If you already pay for ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, or API usage, this can show whether your stack is efficient.”

I would comment under posts where people discuss AI API costs, tool subscriptions, or startup burn. The goal would not be to spam links, but to add useful context and offer the audit when relevant.

A realistic 30-day target:

- 300 landing page visitors
- 100 audit starts
- 60 completed audits
- 20 saved reports
- 5–8 user conversations
- 2–3 high-savings leads worth deeper follow-up

## Unfair Distribution Channel

The unfair distribution loop is the public shareable audit report. Each completed audit creates a page that can be shared with a cofounder, engineering team, finance person, or online audience. If one founder shares a report showing meaningful savings, other teams with similar AI stacks may want to run their own audit.

Another advantage is public teardown content. AI tool spending is still new and confusing for many small teams, so practical examples are more useful than generic content marketing.

## Week-1 Traction Target

If this works, week-1 traction should look like:

- 50 landing page visitors
- 20 audit starts
- 12 completed audits
- 5 saved reports
- 3 direct user feedback conversations
- 1 public report shared by someone other than me

If completed audits are low, the form is too long or unclear. If saved reports are low, the result page is not valuable enough. If users complete audits but do not share or save them, the recommendations may need to become more specific and trustworthy.
